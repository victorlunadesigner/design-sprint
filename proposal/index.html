Overview

What this sprint is

A focused, async four-week engagement to design one complete, shippable user flow — from research inputs to a high-fidelity prototype ready for development or user testing.

What you get

  • One user flow designed end-to-end with wireframes, UI, and interactive prototype
  • Validated screen inventory so nothing important is missed in future phases
  • Dev-ready handoff with specs, states, and a documented flow map

What this is not

  • A full product redesign or complete screen library
  • A design system build or brand identity project
  • Multiple flows in a single sprint
After the sprint: Additional user flows are delivered through a monthly retainer — one sprint cycle per flow, at a reduced rate.

How we'll work

  • Async-first — Loom updates, written feedback, shared Figma files
  • Two feedback rounds per week, max
  • 48-hour response window on your side to keep the timeline on track
  • Single decision-maker — or one named tie-breaker

Delivery

Step 0

Product snapshot

Tell us what you're building and list every screen the product will need — even ones outside this sprint.

What we collect

  • Product name, one-liner, stage (idea / MVP / live), 90-day goal
  • What's built vs. planned today
  • Full screen inventory tagged Must, Should, or Later (MoSCoW)

Why it matters

The screen inventory gives us a complete product map upfront. We design one flow in the sprint, but nothing launch-critical gets missed when we plan later phases.

Step 1

Ideal customer profile

The specific person who gets the most value from the flow we're designing. One flow, one ICP.

What we collect

  • Context, role, age range
  • Goals, aspirations, frustrations, limitations
  • Alternatives they use today and the trigger moment

Why it matters

Design decisions get tested against one real person, not a vague "users" group. Every screen serves their goal.

Example — Toggl Track (Creating a project flow)

LimitationsNon-technical, 2 min max to set up a new project

Step 2

Jobs to be done

List the outcomes users hire your product for — not features. Distribute 10 points across jobs. Only one job can be level 3.

What we collect

  • 3–5 jobs written as: "When I…, I want to…, so I can…"
  • Importance score (1–3) with a 10-point budget
  • Pain level (1–5) and who is trying to do each job

Why it matters

The point cap forces prioritization. The level-3 job becomes the anchor for flow selection — we design what matters most first.

Example — Toggl Track

Step 3

Flow selection

Pick one user flow for this sprint. It should map to your highest-priority job (level 3).

What we collect

  • Chosen flow name and which JTBD it serves
  • Entry point — how the user starts
  • Success metric — how you'll know it worked
The sprint designs screens within this flow only. Must screens outside this flow stay on the backlog for the monthly retainer.

Example — Toggl Track

Chosen flowCreating a project
Tied to JTBDOrganize time by project for accurate billing
Entry pointSidebar → Projects → Create project button
Success metricUser creates a project and logs first time entry in under 3 minutes

Step 4

Flow map

Map the steps a user takes to reach one end goal. Actions and decisions — not screens. Max 12 steps.

What we collect

  • End goal (one sentence)
  • Numbered steps: user action → system response
  • Optional branches, known pain points, out-of-scope items

Example — Toggl Track: Creating a project

End goal: Project created with team invited, ready to track time.

Step 5

Constraints & assets

Everything needed to design without back-and-forth. Skip what doesn't apply.

What we collect

  • Brand assets (logo, colors, fonts) or permission to propose
  • Existing UI — Figma links, screenshots, product URL
  • Platform (web, iOS, both) and auth model
  • 2–3 competitor or reference links
  • Decision-maker name and feedback SLA (e.g. 48 hours)
  • Legal / compliance requirements if any

Why it matters

Collected once, upfront. Prevents mid-sprint delays waiting for brand files or platform decisions.
