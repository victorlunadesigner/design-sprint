Overview

What this sprint is

A focused, async four-week engagement to design one complete, shippable user flow for SerVme V2 — from your existing product context and knowledge base to a high-fidelity prototype ready for React + MUI development.

What you get

  • One user flow designed end-to-end with wireframes, UI, and interactive prototype
  • Validated screen inventory so nothing important is missed in future phases
  • Dev-ready handoff with specs, states, and a documented flow map

What this is not

  • A full product redesign or complete screen library
  • A design system build or brand identity project
  • Multiple flows in a single sprint
Phased delivery: Your Reservations Dashboard spans multiple views (List, Floor Plan, Grid, Cover Flow). We tackle one flow per sprint — starting with the highest-impact workflow — then continue through a monthly retainer at a reduced rate per flow.

How we'll work

  • Async-first — Loom updates, written feedback, shared Figma files
  • Two feedback rounds per week, max
  • 48-hour response window on your side to keep the timeline on track
  • Single decision-maker — or one named tie-breaker

Step 1

Ideal customer profile

The specific person who gets the most value from the flow we're designing. One flow, one ICP.

What we collect

  • Context, role, age range
  • Goals, aspirations, frustrations, limitations
  • Alternatives they use today and the trigger moment

Why it matters

Design decisions get tested against one real person, not a vague "users" group. Every screen serves their goal.

Example — SerVme (Adding a reservation flow)

ContextFront-of-house hostess at a multi-section restaurant (dining area + terrace) during a busy lunch shift
RoleHostess / reservations coordinator — uses the dashboard all day, every day
Age22–35
GoalsSeat guests on time, never miss allergies or VIP tags, keep tables turning
FrustrationsToo many columns to scan, guest tags hidden behind clicks, unclear late-arrival indicators
LimitationsOne hand on the phone, 30 seconds max to add a walk-in during service
TriggerGuest calls to book for tonight — needs party breakdown, table, and payment info fast

Step 2

Jobs to be done

List the outcomes users hire your product for — not features. Distribute 10 points across jobs. Only one job can be level 3.

What we collect

  • 3–5 jobs written as: "When I…, I want to…, so I can…"
  • Importance score (1–3) with a 10-point budget
  • Pain level (1–5) and who is trying to do each job

Why it matters

The point cap forces prioritization. The level-3 job becomes the anchor for flow selection — we design what matters most first.

Example — SerVme V2

Job to be doneScorePainWho
When service starts, I want to scan all reservations at a glance, so I can spot late arrivals and seat guests on time 35Hostess / FOH manager
When a guest calls or walks in, I want to add a reservation with party breakdown and table, so nothing gets missed 24Hostess / reservations staff
When tables overlap, I want to resolve conflicts in one action, so the floor keeps moving 24FOH manager
When a guest arrives, I want to update status and seated count instantly, so the kitchen and waitstaff stay in sync 13Hostess

Points used: 8 / 10 — one level-3 job selected.

Step 4

Flow map

Map the steps a user takes to reach one end goal. Actions and decisions — not screens. Max 12 steps.

What we collect

  • End goal (one sentence)
  • Numbered steps: user action → system response
  • Optional branches, known pain points, out-of-scope items

Everything needed to design without back-and-forth. Skip what doesn't apply.
